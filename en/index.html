Welcome

Bleaching, dyeing and finishing — done to the highest standard.

Johann Müller AG specializes in the bleaching, dyeing and finishing of yarns, jersey, terry and garments from all dyeable materials. Our products are of excellent quality and enjoy a high reputation on the market — our customers include leading international textile and clothing companies.

With a dyeing capacity of over 20,000 kg per day, we are one of the most powerful yarn and jersey finishing companies in Switzerland. We focus on reliability, quality, advanced technology and a rapid delivery service.

Products & Services

Two plants. One standard of precision.

Yarn Dyeing

A modern, fully automatic crosswound bobbin dyeing plant with a dyeing capacity of about 10,000 kg of yarn per day.

  • Crosswound bobbin dyeing — 5 kg to 1,000 kg batches, ~10,000 kg/day
  • Hank dyeing by spray and cabinet machines — 10 kg to 300 kg batches, ~1,500 kg/day
  • Winding, rewinding and waxing into conical, cylindrical and pineapple bobbins
  • Twisting before or after dyeing

Piece Dyeing

A modern, fully automatic jet dyeing plant with a dyeing capacity of around 10,000 kg per day, plus dedicated garment dyeing.

  • HT and atmospheric jet dyeing — 5 kg to 700 kg batches, ~10,000 kg/day
  • Traditional reel dyeing for delicate textiles, and terry-fabric machines
  • Garment dyeing up to 75 kg batches
  • Finishing up to 2.40 m wide — pile raising, shearing, grinding

Materials: Cotton, viscose, linen, wool, silk, polyester, polyamide, polyacrylonitrile and blends thereof — staple-fibre and filament yarns.

History

Five generations, one family business.

From a farmhouse printing mill in 1845 to one of Switzerland's most powerful yarn and jersey finishing operations — a timeline shaped by the Müller family.

  1. 1845

    A farmhouse mill

    Foundation of a yarn and cotton fabric printing mill in a farmhouse in Strengelbach. Manual weaving dominates the lower Wiggertal region.

  2. 1883

    A dyeing facility

    Following Johann Müller-Wullschleger's death, his son Johann Paul Müller joins management and establishes a yarn bleaching and dyeing facility beside the printing mill.

  3. 1892

    Relocation

    The business relocates 300 metres south to its current site, next to a crucial water source.

  4. 1899

    Machine dyeing

    Conversion from manual to machine dyeing. Products exported to British India, Southeast Asia, the Philippines and Turkey.

  5. 1917

    Streba knitwear

    A knitting plant and ready-made clothing manufacture are incorporated under the brand name Streba. World War I interrupts export.

  6. 1927

    Six sons take over

    Johann Paul Müller transfers management to his six sons. The company becomes "Johann Müller Aktiengesellschaft – Färberei und Strickerei," reaching 310 employees.

  7. 1945

    100 years

    The company marks its centenary with 540 employees, run by five sons across commercial, technical, knitting, sales and dyeing divisions.

  8. 1968

    Fourth generation

    Dr. Kurt and Werner Müller, sons of Werner Müller Sr., take control of the Strengelbach dyeing facility.

  9. 1978

    First of its kind

    Johann Müller AG develops the first double-band nozzle shrinking dryer for low-shrinkage finishing without chemical additives — now a worldwide standard.

  10. 1979–1999

    Growth by acquisition

    Six finishing and dyeing operations join the group across Bürglen, Basel, Oberuzwil, Anglikon, Gais, Birrwil, Zofingen, Roggwil and Küsnacht.

  11. 1993

    World-first certification

    As the world's first textile finishing company, Johann Müller AG is certified to OEKO-TEX® Standard 100.

  12. 2001

    Renewable energy pioneer

    Thermal energy generation shifts from oil to bone grease. The effort earns the Organic Textile Award and the Swiss Solarpreis.

  13. 2002

    Fifth generation

    Markus Müller, son of Dr. Kurt Müller, joins the company.

  14. 2006–2008

    SwissCotton

    With spinning mill Hermann Bühler AG, Johann Müller AG launches SwissCotton Rainbow and SwissCotton BeDry — innovative, moisture-transporting natural yarns.
